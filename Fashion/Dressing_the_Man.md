# Dressing The Man

## Proportions
### The Suit jacket
#### Shoulder
- Most important dimension of the jacket. A tailor cannot fix the jacket with an incorrect shoulder dimension.
- Too narrow = larger head
- Too wide = smaller head
- Never wear sharply angled shoulders

#### Jacket Length
- Enough to cover the curvature of the buttocks while giving the leg as long a line as possible.
- Can vary up to $\frac{1}{2}$ inch without missing its longevity
- It is normal for a jacket to be slightly longer in front vs back so that it can hang properly.
- **General Rule**: Jacket's bottom should line up with thumb knuckle.

#### Waist Button
- Raise it too much and the torso becomes abbreviated
- Lower it too much and the torso is elongated at the expense of a longer leg line
- When fastened, it should divide the body so that the torso and legs appears at max length.
- Correct placement is at $\frac{1}{2}$ inch below the natural waist
    - **Finding natural waist**: Place your hands to the smallest part of the torso and then press down at the sides into the hollow above the hip-bone.

#### Gorge and Lapel Width
- The positioning of the Gorge should be a function of one's build, not fashion.
- If a man is short, a man's lapel notches should sit higher up on his chest, the longer lapel line emphasizing verticality.
- The signle-breasted lapel should cover $\frac{2}{5}$ to $\frac{3}{5}$ of the distance between the jacket's chest and shoulder line

#### Jacket Sleeve
- Full at the top, tapered down to the wrist bone
- Converging lines conform to shoulder and narrowing waist of the jacket
- **0.5 to 1.25 cm** of shirt cuff showing
- When you’re wearing a watch, the sleeve should **naturally rise slightly** when your arm bends, allowing the watch to peek out—*not* constantly cover it.
- Sleeve opening shpuld be wide enough to comfortably pass over your watch without catching
- Enough fabric to glide over the watch when you move, but not too much that it becomes bell shaped.

### The Suit Trouser
- Should be worn on the waist, not on the hip.
- The **break** is the crease or fold where your trouser hem meets your shoe.
    - **No break** (full break avoidance)
        - Hem just kisses the top of your shoe, no crease
        - Very clean, modern look
        - Works best with slim or tailored fits
        - Shows more sock when you move
    - **Quarter break** (recommended default)
        - Slight crease at the front, minimal stacking
        - Arguably the most versatile and balanced option
        - Clean without looking trendy or extreme
    - **Half break** 
        - Noticeable crease, slight stacking
        - Traditional and safe
        - Works well for business settings
    - **Full break**
        - Deep crease with stacking over the shoe
        - More old-school; can look heavy if not styled carefully

### The Dress Shirt
- White, Long-Sleeve (Absolute essential)
- Light blue (power blue)
- Subtle stripe
- Oxford button-down for more caual

### Necktie
- Should adhere to the dimension of the lapel. Wider lapel = wider tie and vice versa.
- Colors:
    - Red - Power/ authority
    - Blue - Trust/calm/reliability
    - Yellow/Gold - Optimism, visibility
    - Green - Balance, growth, confidence
    - Pueple - Luxury
    - Black - Formality
    - Off white - Clean
    - Brown - Grounded
- Patterns:
    - Solid: strongest, most direct message
    - Stripes: classic, business-ready
    - Dots: approachable, slightly playful
    - Paisley: expressive, more personality
